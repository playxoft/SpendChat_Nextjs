Most personal finance apps fail at the very first step: actually logging what you spent. The forms are long, the modals are fiddly, and after a few days the habit quietly dies. We started SpendChat from the opposite end — what if adding a transaction felt like sending a text?

## The chat model

In SpendChat your transactions live in a feed, like messages. Income arrives on the left, expenses on the right, each in a tidy bubble with its category and time. To add one you pick expense or income, type an amount, give it a short title, and hit send. That's it — the balance updates and the bubble drops into place.

It sounds small, but the framing changes everything. A chat is something you return to dozens of times a day without thinking. A form is a chore you avoid. By borrowing the mental model of messaging, logging money stops feeling like accounting and starts feeling like a quick note to yourself.

## Fast by default

- Type "/" in the title to tag a category without touching the mouse.
- ⌘/Ctrl + ↵ sends; ⇧ + ↵ opens a description when you need one.
- Day dividers and a running balance keep the whole month readable at a glance.

The result is a tracker you actually keep using past the first week — which, in the end, is the only feature that matters.
